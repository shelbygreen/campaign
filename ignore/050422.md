may 4, 2022

earlier, i was at dinner with a good friend and we were discussing the impact of roe v wade. it made me recall how i used to feel when i had sex with someone, my period came late, and then i started overthinking about what would happen if i was pregnant and id have to get an abortion. i've never had to get an abortion, but i know my mom has. and got one when she was a teenager. i think that's part of the reason why she didn't introduce me to birth control. she thought it would be a gateway to having sex at an early age and possibly making the same mistke she did. 

roe v wade hasn't been overturned yet, but the lack of response or outrage is telling. part of me thinks discourse, offline, is muted because people are tired of the unwarranted and common attacks on women's rights. but i also think it's because people just don't know how to bring it up or who to bring it up to. 

if i was in a different stage in life, i would've reacted differently. it maybe would've forced me to access safer forms of birth control. 

but when i was a college student, i relied on the "pull out" method and period tracking to prevent pregnancy. now, people are advised to delete their period tracking app because their data is being stolen... 

i genuinely do not understand. why is the responsibility constantly shifting to the individual, to the consumer, to protect themselves against giant corporations? why is there no accountability or just this wave of acceptable complacency. 

shifting topics a bit...

i didn't get the endorsement from the tallahassee professional firefighters. i'm a little bummed about it, but it's not the end of the world. i told myself to try my best, and i did exactly that. i asked what metrics they used to score candidates and decide which one to endorse. so i'll get a chance to see how they viewed my candidacy and where there's an avenue to improve. rejection is part of the process.
