title: 'Life on the Bottom'
date: '2023-08-04'
tag: 'personal'
---

I had coffee with Meg earlier. She wanted to get a chance to chat before Benji and I leave Tallahassee. Part of me wanted to cancel because I've been so busy with the move and work, but I decided not to. I'm sure she would've completely understood and even mentioned how tired I must be with all that we having going on. But I decided to honor my plans and see her! 

On the way I passed by the Capitol Inn, which is actually a new development. It used to house a realtor firm, now it's more like a micro-hotel. I liked it!

Before leaving, Meg said that she enjoyed our blossoming friendship and how much she learns from me. She was being so sweet, too. Telling me that I have such a sweet character. I feel like she's helping me lean into my vulnerability. I told her that I did sex work during college and that I wished no one had to experience that. On the walk home, I though about what I told her and how I felt as if I could never tell my parents that. They'd be so upset, hurt, that they didn't know I was in that kind of position. And if I just told them, I'm sure that would've helped in any way they could. I think I pushed them away because I thought I was undeserving of their help. Or that I shouldn't have to need help or have to rely on anyone. I can't say there was an exact time I decided that or developed that way of thinking. I know it's not right and I don't believe that now, but it's making me think if I thought I was deserving of love and if I even knew what love meant back at that age.

But anyway, the reason why I wanted to write this is because at the end of our conversation, I told Meg: "that's what it's like living on the bottom". She told me she learned so much from our chats and it's basically a verbal history of my life as a young, Black woman trying to make a difference in politics and people's lives.
